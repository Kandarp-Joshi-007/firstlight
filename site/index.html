How it works. Every HTTPS certificate issued anywhere is published to public CT logs within seconds. firstlight reads that stream, filters for domains imitating Irish banks, An Post, Revenue, the HSE and others, then asks Jev — a decision-only model returning calibrated probabilities — whether each is really impersonation. Scores at or above 0.70 are flagged; 0.50–0.69 are held for review.

Coverage is partial by design. A full tail of one CT log is roughly 121 GB/day, so this stays at the head of the log and processes what bandwidth allows. It does not see every certificate and does not claim to. Measured recall in calibration was about 22% at 97% precision.

Known limits. Phishing hosted on github.io, repl.co or compromised sites carries no signal in the domain name and is not detectable this way.
